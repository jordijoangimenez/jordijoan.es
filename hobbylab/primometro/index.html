How it is calculated

Risk premium

The difference between a country's 10-year bond yield and Germany's (or the euro area average), in basis points: 1 percentage point = 100 bp. A high premium means the market asks that country for more interest than Germany.

Monthly data

For each country the ECB publishes the monthly average of the 10-year yield (the series for the Maastricht convergence criterion), about a month late. It is not the daily premium quoted in the media, which comes from paid market data providers.

Inflation

Annual rate of Eurostat's harmonised index of consumer prices (HICP), the measure the ECB uses for its 2% target. On the map, warm colours mean inflation above 2% and blue ones below it. The real rate is the deposit rate minus euro area inflation: when it is negative, money on deposit loses purchasing power.

Accumulated inflation

How much prices have risen since the first month of the chosen period, from Eurostat's HICP index (not by adding up annual rates). The deposit line shows how much a deposit paid the ECB deposit rate each month would have grown: when it stays below prices, those savings lost purchasing power.

Public deficit

General government balance as % of GDP (net lending or borrowing), from the excessive deficit notifications Eurostat publishes every April and October. Negative is a deficit; the EU limit is a 3% deficit. The data is yearly: for the months of 2026 the latest published year is shown.

GDP

Annual growth of gross domestic product in volume (after inflation) and accumulated growth since the first year of the period, from Eurostat's chain-linked series. The latest quarter shows growth on the same quarter a year earlier, seasonally adjusted. In Ireland and Malta, GDP is strongly affected by the accounts of large multinationals.

ECB rates

Deposit facility, main refinancing operations and marginal lending facility, on the date each change takes effect. Between 2000 and 2008 the main operations used variable rates; the minimum bid rate is shown.

Before the euro

Until a country adopts the euro, its spread with Germany includes exchange rate risk. That stretch is drawn lighter.

Small countries

In countries with little long-term debt (for example Estonia, Lithuania or Luxembourg) the series relies on few bonds or on approximations, and is not always comparable with the others.

Decisions and premium

The decisions table compares the premium in the month before each rate change with the premium three months later. It shows what happened, not why: many other factors weigh in those months.

Sources: European Central Bank, ECB Data Portal (bond yields and interest rates); Eurostat (HICP inflation; public deficit; GDP). This site is independent and not affiliated with the ECB or Eurostat.
